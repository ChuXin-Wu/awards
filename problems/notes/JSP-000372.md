# JSP-000372: prime-power reduction and finite verification

[Problem record](../catalog-0301-0400.md#JSP-000372) · [Search program](../../scripts/search_lcm_prime_gap.py)

This note proves the exact prime-power reformulation used by the search and
records an independently reproducible finite verification. It does **not**
prove the conjecture for all consecutive primes.

## Statement

Let \(p_k\) be the \(k\)th prime and
\(L(n)=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)\). The question is whether

\[
L(p_{k+1}-1)<p_kL(p_k)
\]

for every \(k\geq1\).

## Prime-power reduction

For every \(n\geq1\), unique factorisation gives

\[
L(n)=\prod_{q\leq n\atop q\ \mathrm{prime}}
q^{\max\{a:q^a\leq n\}}
=\prod_{q^a\leq n\atop q\ \mathrm{prime},\ a\geq1}q.
\]

In the second product, a prime base occurs once for each one of its powers up
to \(n\).

Now let \(p<r\) be consecutive primes. Dividing the two product formulae gives

\[
\frac{L(r-1)}{L(p)}
=\prod_{p<q^a<r\atop q\ \mathrm{prime},\ a\geq1}q.
\]

There is no prime strictly between \(p\) and \(r\), so the terms with \(a=1\)
are absent. Thus

\[
\frac{L(r-1)}{L(p)}
=\prod_{p<q^a<r\atop q\ \mathrm{prime},\ a\geq2}q,
\]

with a base \(q\) counted once for each power of \(q\) in the interval.
Therefore the original inequality for the gap \((p,r)\) is equivalent to the
exact integer comparison

\[
\prod_{p<q^a<r\atop a\geq2}q<p.
\qquad\square
\]

## Search completeness

For a bound \(N\), the program:

1. sieves every prime at most \(N\);
2. generates every prime power \(q^a\leq N\) with \(a\geq2\);
3. groups those powers by their unique consecutive-prime interval; and
4. performs the equivalent integer comparison above.

Hence a run with bound \(N\) checks every gap whose right-hand prime is at most
\(N\). The unit test independently computes direct least common multiples for
all gaps through 10,000 and confirms that their quotients equal the reduced
products.

## Reproducible result

The repository run with \(N=10^8\) checked 5,761,454 consecutive-prime gaps and
found no counterexample. The largest checked prime was 99,999,989. The closest
case was the gap \((7,11)\): it contains \(8=2^3\) and \(9=3^2\), so the
quotient is \(2\cdot3=6<7\).

From the repository root:

```console
python scripts/search_lcm_prime_gap.py --limit 100000000
```

This is a finite verification. It does not address the obstruction at
arbitrarily large prime gaps and therefore does not change the problem's open
status.
